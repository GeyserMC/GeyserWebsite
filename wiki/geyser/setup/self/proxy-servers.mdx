:::info
- Install Geyser **only** on the proxy server! You can install Floodgate on all servers for better skin support & network-wide Floodgate API availability for other plugins.   
- All servers on the network must accept <Versions.java/> Java clients, as Geyser imitates one.  
:::

<SetupSteps>
<Step id="installing-geyser" title={<MDXTranslatable.setup.installing_geyser />}>

- Download the plugin for your proxy software from [the download page](/download).
- Place the Geyser jar in the `plugins` folder.
- Restart the server.

</Step>
<Step id="configuring-geyser" title={<MDXTranslatable.setup.configuring_geyser />}>

<SelfHostConfig configLocation="/plugins/Geyser-xyz/config.yml" />

Since you are self-hosting, you can choose the port freely - the default port is 19132. Geyser uses it over both TCP and UDP, so it cannot be the proxy's port.
**Important**: Other services/plugins that rely on UDP ports, such as Voice Chats or Query, cannot share the port with Geyser.

</Step>
<Step id="connecting-on-bedrock" title={<MDXTranslatable.setup.connecting_on_bedrock />}>

**Connecting locally in the same network**  
On the same device as the server, you can connect using `localhost`, or `127.0.0.1` as the address.
Other devices in the same local network can use your local IPv4 to connect - it starts with `10.` or `192.168.`.

**Connecting from a different network**  
You will need to expose the port Geyser runs on to the Internet if you want players from outside your network to join.

:::caution
The Geyser port has to be forwarded for **both TCP and UDP**. Before NetherNet, forwarding UDP was enough. Now, players use TCP to find your server, and UDP to play on it, so without TCP your server will not show up.
If you use the "NetherNet and RakNet" option, also forward the second port (`webrtc-port`) for UDP.
:::

To achieve that, you have two options:

- Port forwarding: Open the Geyser port (e.g. 19132) for **both TCP and UDP** in your router/modem, and in the Windows/Linux firewall.
  [Our port forwarding guide](/wiki/geyser/port-forwarding) explains how to do this in detail.
  Then, players can connect with your public IPv4 + port to your server.
- playit.gg: Instead of opening a port (which might not be an option/if you do not want to expose your home ip), you can use
  the playit.gg service to create a tunnel for you to route the traffic through. See our [playit.gg guide](/wiki/geyser/playit-gg).
  With NetherNet, the tunnel has to carry both TCP and UDP, and its public address has to be listed under `advertise-addresses` in the `builtin` part of the `signaling` section.
  Ngrok will not work since it does not support UDP.
